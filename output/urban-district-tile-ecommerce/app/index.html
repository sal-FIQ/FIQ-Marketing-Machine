01

The opportunity

Urban District is a pure-play tile e-commerce platform — 10,000+ SKUs, the "world's largest selection" — disrupting a category still run through showrooms and distributors. It serves two very different buyers: builders & contractors (≈60–65% of category volume) and homeowners & designers (higher margin).

The growth engine hinges on one mechanic: the free sample program (up to 25/week). Get a sample in someone's hands and the path to purchase opens. So the whole performance-marketing job reduces to two compounding levers — drive more (right-fit) sample requests, and convert more samples into orders — run differently for builders vs. designers.

The thesis in one line

Treat the sample request as the macro top-of-funnel conversion (like an "add to cart"), then engineer a measured nurture + retargeting system that moves samples to purchase — segmented by builder vs. designer, and tied directly to revenue. It's the same dynamic I scaled at Canon ($19M e-store, +40%); the tile catalog just changes the creative and the segmentation.

Cheat sheet

Private

Review 5 minutes before. Tone: calm, sharp, concise, commercially aware — a peer conversation, not an audition.

Executive phrases

"The way I'd think about that is…" "I'd break that into two parts…" "What I've learned building these systems is…" "The real leverage point in e-commerce like this is…" "If I were stepping into this role, I'd start with…"

Traps to avoid

I joined intending to stay long-term, and the work delivered — marketing drove ~55% of net-new revenue. What became clear is that the operating environment limited my ability to compound that impact. At this stage I'm deliberately optimizing for an environment where I can commit for several years and build something durable.
